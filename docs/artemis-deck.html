01 — Problem

CI failure triage is high-volume, low-complexity toil.

Every engineering team runs continuous integration, and a large share of what it reports is mechanical to diagnose: a dependency drifted, a flaky test re-ran, a migration ordered wrong, a cache key went stale.

Diagnosing these is not hard. It is tedious, it interrupts whoever is on call, and it scales linearly with the number of pipelines a team runs.

02 — Why that market is blocked

The tools that automate it require source-code egress.

Existing assistants send source code and build logs to a third-party model API. For a large class of buyer, that single architectural fact disqualifies the entire category:

Regulated industries

Finance, healthcare, and critical infrastructure operate under audit regimes that treat third-party model inference as uncontrolled data processing.

Defence & public sector

Suppliers working under export control or classification cannot route source artefacts through a commercial inference endpoint.

Source-code escrow

Contractual custody obligations are incompatible with transmitting code to a provider the customer has no privity with.

06 — The constraint

Every run to date has been on a free Tesla T4.

  • T4 is Turing (SM 7.5). No native bfloat16 — is_bf16_supported() returns true, but the path is emulated, so training runs in fp16 with loss scaling.
  • 16 GB of VRAM caps us near 150M parameters. Our last attempt at a larger batch returned CUDA OOM at 14.56 GiB.
  • Single device. The FSDP sharding path cannot be validated at all, because there is no second GPU to shard across.
What Ampere-or-newer unlocks

bf16 tensor cores (SM ≥ 8.0) — numerically stable training without loss scaling.

Multi-GPU interconnect — validates the sharded path that a 1–3B model requires.

TensorRT / Triton — quantised serving, which is a direct margin driver for a self-hosted product.

A100-class access is the difference between a 153M research artefact and a 1–3B production model. It is the single gating dependency on this project.

07 — Market

DevOps tooling, entered through the segment that cannot buy the incumbents.

Wedge

No-egress software delivery

Teams for whom third-party model inference is disqualifying, not merely undesirable. They are underserved precisely because the constraint is architectural and cannot be retrofitted by a competitor built on a hosted API.

Why a small model is the right answer here

Narrow task, structured context

CI triage has bounded inputs — a log, a diff, a test report. Cost per triaged failure, not general capability, is the metric buyers compare. A specialised 1–3B model competes on that metric in a way a frontier model's pricing does not.

We are pre-revenue and make no forecast here. The claim on this slide is about why the segment exists and why it is structurally hard for an API-based competitor to serve — not about captured demand.
